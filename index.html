The months bonus

Two free shares in total, not two per share held. For someone holding the minimum ten, that's a 20% bonus. For a large holder it rounds to nothing.

You buy in naira and live in shillings. If the naira weakens against the shilling you lose value even if the Lagos price never moves. Dollar dividends were announced but still awaiting Nigerian regulatory approval — treat that as intended, not settled.

Can you actually buy it?

This depends on your passport, not where you live. Almost nobody answers it straight.

The words you'll meet

BVN — Bank Verification Number. Nigeria's banking ID. Citizens and residents only. Without it, no retail broker there can open your account.

CSCS account — where Nigerian shares live electronically. Your broker opens it for you.

CDS account — the Kenyan equivalent, for NSE shares. A licensed broker opens it in one to three days, usually free.

CCI — Certificate of Capital Importation. Proof your foreign currency entered Nigeria legally. Without it, getting your money back out later is difficult. This is the one people skip and regret.

Open letter · Nairobi, September 2026

Dear Aliko Dangote

You have told the press this is the IPO of the people, and that you want ten million retail investors. I want you to have them. I also want to tell you, from Nairobi, exactly how you are going to miss.

In January this year my government sold 65% of Kenya Pipeline — KES 106.3 billion, the largest share sale in East African history. It targeted two million Kenyan investors.

Seventy-three thousand came. Three and a half per cent of the target.

Kenyan individuals took19% of their quota
Foreign investors took0.2% of theirs
Left unclaimedKES 59 billion
Bought instead byPension funds and Uganda

Understand what was not the problem. You could apply by USSD. You could pay by M-Pesa. There was an electronic portal and a fintech front-end. Shares now trade inside the M-Pesa app with no depository account at all. Every rail people said was missing had been built.

In 2008 Safaricom drew over eight hundred thousand Kenyans with paper forms and bank queues. In 2026, with all of that technology, we drew seventy-three thousand. Access improved and participation fell nine in ten.

What this means for you

Your prospectus names an African distribution channel. Kenya reserved a fifth of its offer for East Africans and that tranche filled — with Ugandan state oil money and Rwandan pension funds. Sovereign capital crosses the border easily. Retail capital has no road at all.

And for us it is worse than a road that is rough. A Kenyan passport holder cannot obtain a Nigerian bank verification number. Without one, no Nigerian broker can open an account. Your offer is not difficult for Kenyans. It is closed.

So when 13 October comes and the retail number is counted, it will be Nigerians and the Nigerian diaspora. East Africa will be a rounding error, and the headline will say the continent turned out for you. It will not be true.

What we are doing about it

This page exists because nobody in Kenya explains a share offer in shillings, tells people plainly whether they are eligible, or publishes afterwards how the last listing actually performed. We do all three. We hold no money, execute nothing and advise no one — we assemble the demand and hand it to people licensed to serve it.

We are building a register of Kenyans who would buy if a compliant route existed. Every name carries an amount. When it is large enough it goes to a Kenyan bank and a licensed broker, and it becomes a business case rather than a request.

What we ask

  1. Name the African channel. Say which East African intermediaries can take applications, or say plainly that none can. Either answer is more use than silence.
  2. Publish the retail split by country when the offer closes. If East Africa is a rounding error, let us all see it.
  3. Talk to whoever builds the retail layer here before your next raise, or before NNPC. It will not build itself, and Nigeria will have the same problem in reverse when it wants East African money.

The refinery is a serious thing and I hope it does what you say it will. I would simply rather my neighbours owned a piece of it than read about it.

Alvin Mutuma
